Business insights and recommendations

Computed on the full, unfiltered dataset.

  • Revenue is concentrated in a small group. Champions are 17.3% of customers (1,019) but produce 65.7% of transaction revenue (11.41M), with an average order of 560 against 321 to 327 for Regular and At Risk. The top 5% of customers account for 52.0% of revenue. Recommendation: protect this group with early access, account management and retention offers.
  • Nearly half the customer base is At Risk. 2,744 customers (46.7%) are At Risk and hold 12.0% of revenue (2.09M). In this workbook the churn flag equals recency over 90 days and all 2,744 carry it, so At Risk and churned describe the same customers here. Recommendation: run a tiered win-back campaign, weighting spend toward those with higher frequency or monetary scores.
  • Loyal customers are lapsing. 219 of 334 Loyal customers (65.6%) are flagged churned despite an average F score of 4.8, and the group holds 10.6% of revenue. Recommendation: contact them first; a lapse in a frequent buyer is a stronger warning than in an occasional one.
  • Revenue is strongly seasonal. September to November is the peak: November 2010 reached 1.17M and November 2011 1.16M, against 0.45M to 0.70M in the other full months. December 2009 and December 2011 are partial. Recommendation: time retention campaigns and stock planning ahead of September.
  • The business depends on the United Kingdom. It provides 82.8% of transaction revenue; EIRE (3.5%), Netherlands (3.2%), Germany (2.4%) and France (2.0%) follow. Recommendation: test targeted campaigns in the nearest European markets before wider expansion.
  • Data-quality caution. Two single lines (168,469.60 and 77,183.60, together 1.41% of revenue) belong to customers whose Monetary is near zero or negative, which suggests the orders were cancelled; this is an inference. Excluding them leaves the segment shares almost unchanged (Champions 66.6% instead of 65.7%).

Assumptions and limitations

  • Definitions. Transaction revenue is the workbook's Revenue column (Quantity × Price, identical to Sales), summed per purchase line and gross. Orders are distinct invoice numbers, counted once per customer, month and country. Customers are distinct IDs with a purchase in the current view. Customer-level fields repeat on every row, so they are read once per customer and never summed.
  • Returns. The file has no cancellations or negative quantities. Monetary and Frequency differ from revenue and invoice counts for 2,511 customers and Monetary is negative for 27. This pattern fits returns that were removed from the rows, but the workbook does not document it. RFM scores and segments are used as provided and not recalculated.
  • Currency. The workbook does not state one, so amounts use a neutral label by default. The public Online Retail II dataset (UCI) has the same date range (1 Dec 2009 to 9 Dec 2011) and a similar structure, and documents its prices in sterling (£). Whether this workbook is that dataset, unconverted, is not confirmed. The £ and $ options only change the displayed symbol; nothing is converted.
  • Customer count. This workbook has 5,881 distinct customer IDs. An earlier analysis reported 5,942, and the workbook cannot explain the 61-customer gap. The UCI dataset lists 1,067,371 rows against 779,486 here, so rows were removed before this file, but which rows and customers is unknown without the original file or the earlier customer list. This is unverified.
  • Segments and dates. Segments are one snapshot per customer, so the date filter shows who bought in the window under their current segment. Filtering is by month, and the first and last months are partial. At Risk equals churned here because the churn flag is recency over 90 days. 59% of customers' Recency values do not match days since their last purchase in the file as of 10 Dec 2011, for a reason I could not determine.
  • Other data points. Country is the transaction country; 13 customers bought from several, and the customer table shows the one with most revenue. 88 zero-price lines add no revenue. Two pairs of lines (1,103.70 in total) may be duplicates and were kept. Manual, postage and similar non-product lines were not excluded.
